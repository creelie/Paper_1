**Abstract (plain text):**

We prove that the ratio of the Grundy domination number of a strong product
to the product of the Grundy domination numbers of its factors is unbounded
over nonempty finite graphs. For each integer m >= 5, we construct two graphs
on the five-element subsets of an m-element set. Their Grundy domination
numbers are at most m and binom(m,2), whereas their strong product has an
independent set of size binom(m,5). Incidence matrices over two finite fields
give a self-contained proof. We also determine the Grundy domination number
of the graph on {0,...,N-1} with adjacency |u-v| congruent to 2 modulo 3:
it is floor((N+1)/3)+3 for every N >= 6. An explicit legal sequence in its
product with the octahedron gives a counterexample on 114 vertices to the
strong-product multiplicativity conjecture. Both general results have
elementary proofs independent of computation. A separate Lean certificate
concerns one finite example.
